# Amanda's Decision Lab 🧠

### A small personal experiment about making decisions when internships, jobs, and the future feel uncertain.

I'm a Year 2 undergrad currently looking for my next summer internship, and honestly, I'm pretty anxious about it :(

I keep seeing internship applications open earlier and earlier, especially for big companies. When I see a rolling application, I start thinking:

* Am I applying early enough? (Honestly, I don't think I am at this point.)
* Am I qualified enough for this position? Do I have the technical skills, projects, and other things that companies are looking for?
* Should I spend more time learning and building a solid project before I start applying? (My situation..)
* Is everyone else already much more prepared than me?
* Am I one of the students who didn't realize that internship recruiting can start almost a year in advance?
* What if I make the wrong decision?
* What if I fail? What if something goes wrong with the intern/job and the future...?

And I know I'm probably not the only student who feels this way.

So instead of only thinking about these questions, I wanted to try something different: **collect some data about my own decisions and see what actually happens.**

I know this data won't explain everyone's anxiety or how everyone makes decisions. There are so many things that can affect the way we think, including experiences from childhood, financial insecurity, family background, and many other things that I can't measure here.

But I think there is still something interesting about looking at **how I perceive uncertainty and anxiety at a specific moment in my life**, especially as a university student.

This project is my personal experiment in understanding how I make decisions when the future is uncertain.

---

## What is Decision Lab?

Decision Lab is a small web application where I can record important decisions **before I make them**, including how uncertain I feel, how important the decision seems, how much research I have done, and what I predict will happen.

Later, I can come back and record what actually happened.

The goal is not to prove that I have found a universal rule for decision-making. It's simply a way for me to look back at my own decisions and ask:

> **Was the situation actually as uncertain or important as I thought it was at the time?**

I also want to see whether the things I worry about actually happen as often as I expect them to.

## My First Decision

My first decision is about internship applications.

**Decision:**
Should I apply to internships such as Morgan Stanley, National Bank, SAP, and other large companies?

**Category:** Career

**How important does this feel?**
8/10

It feels urgent because some internships recruit on a rolling basis, and I worry that if I wait too long, there may not be many positions left.

**How uncertain do I feel?**
10/10

**How many options am I considering?**
A lot. Right now, I'm considering many different companies and internship directions rather than one fixed path.

**How much time have I spent researching?**
About 1 hour per day.

**What is making me uncertain?**

I'm worried that I'm not qualified enough for many of the internship descriptions I see. I don't have all of the technical skills listed in the JDs, and I don't feel like I have enough impressive projects yet.

At the same time, I'm worried that if I spend too much time building projects and learning before applying, some rolling applications might close.

So I'm stuck between two thoughts:

> **"I should apply now."**

and

> **"I should become more qualified first."**

**What do I currently predict will happen?**

I currently expect that I may not receive many interviews or even hear back from some companies because I don't think my current skills and projects are strong enough.

**How confident am I in this prediction?**
8/10

This is my starting point.

I'll come back later to see what actually happens.

## Questions I Want to Explore

As I collect more decisions, I want to explore questions such as:

1. Does spending more time researching actually make me more confident?
2. Do decisions with more options make me feel more uncertain?
3. Do I tend to overestimate how important a decision will be?
4. Do I tend to underestimate my own ability?
5. For things like internship applications, how does the number of interviews or opportunities I actually receive compare with what I expected?
6. How accurate are my predictions about what will happen?
7. Does my confidence change after I actually make a decision?
8. Are there patterns between different types of decisions?

## Why am I building this?

Partly because I want to learn how to build a real application. But there's also a more personal reason.

I feel like anxiety about the future has become something a lot of university students experience, especially now that AI is changing the way we think about school, careers, and even whether the skills we are learning will still be valuable in a few years.

Sometimes I catch myself thinking about the future so much that I forget to look at what is actually happening right now.

So I wanted to take something that feels very personal and uncertain to me and turn it into something I can **observe, record, and learn from**.

I also know that not everything about psychology or human decision-making can be represented by numbers. A number like "8/10 anxiety" obviously can't describe everything someone is feeling.

But maybe the number can still capture **how I perceived the situation at that moment**.

And maybe, after collecting enough decisions, I can look back and ask:

**Was I actually as uncertain as I thought I was?**

**Did the things I was afraid of actually happen?**

**Do I consistently underestimate myself?** :(

Maybe the data will prove some of my assumptions right.

Maybe it won't.

That's kind of the point.
